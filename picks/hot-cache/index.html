XI.5

The hot cache, replayed

The first reading of the replay was registered as a calibration. If the stand-in’s miss rates came within a factor of two of HiSparse’s, its other readings would be believed. They came within 1.2–1.5×, with the same slope and the same gap to a clairvoyant policy. Then the misses were counted per step and per layer.

HiSparse measured its hot cache on one GLM-5.1 trace: 30, 13.4 and 6.7 % of picks missed with 2048, 4096 and 8192 slots for 2048 picks per layer per step (III.9 The Lucky Louis property). The plan wrote down, before any data, what a stand-in’s numbers would have to look like to be believed: miss rates within a factor of two of these (X.9 What each outcome would mean).

Calibration against HiSparse

The replay ran HiSparse’s LRU with its guard on the stand-in’s picks, for the three named configurations of XI.4 › The named configurations, at the same ratios B/k HiSparse used.

configuration B/k = 1 2 4 Bélády at 2 consecutive-step overlap grade
HiSparse, GLM-5.1 (2048 picks of 100K) 30 % 13.4 % 6.7 % 8.2 % ≈ 70 % their measurement
pin-scaled (512 picks, W = 0) 35.1 [34.4–36.2] 19.0 [18.2–20.2] 9.7 [8.7–10.7] 12.0 65 % measured
pin-absolute (2048 picks, W = 0) 28.1 10.0 2.5 5.5 72 % measured
v4-scaled (128 squeezed rows of 4, W = 32) 33.4 17.9 9.6 11.6 67 % measured

Read the pin-scaled row against HiSparse’s.

  1. The 2× rule holds at every size: 1.17, 1.41 and 1.45 times HiSparse’s rates. Pin-absolute fails it at B/k=4 (0.37×). That failure is what the next page predicts: 2048 of about 27K positions is a budget share of 7.5 %, not 2 % (XI.6 The share carries, not the budget).
  2. The same slope. Over B/k from 1 to 8 the miss rate falls as a power of the cache size, m∝B−α, with α=1.07 [1.01–1.15] for pin-scaled against 1.08 for HiSparse’s three points (III.10 One curve behind every cache number).
  3. The same gap to a clairvoyant policy. Bélády at B=2k misses 12.0 %; LRU over Bélády is 1.58, against HiSparse’s 13.4 / 8.2 = 1.63.
  4. Slightly more churn. The Lucky Louis property, read as the share of a step’s picks that the previous step also picked: 65 % for pin-scaled, about 70 % for GLM-5.1. GLM-5.1’s trained indexer re-picks more than exact attention does on Qwen 2.5 1.5B (XI.12 What the indexer can change gives one reason this can happen).
☞ Things to try
  1. Choose pin-scaled. The line runs just above HiSparse’s diamonds, parallel to the grey fit.
  2. Choose pin-absolute. Its line falls much more steeply: 2048 picks of about 27K positions are a share of 7.5 %, and the next page shows the slope rising with the share (0.63 at 64 picks, 2.2 at 2048).
Running themeTrust, but shuffle

The 2× rule was written into X.9 What each outcome would mean before the first replay ran. A stand-in that missed four times more often than GLM-5.1, or four times less, would have been reported as uncalibrated and every later page read with that caveat. Pin-scaled passed at all three sizes; pin-absolute failed at one, for a reason the next page measures.

Takeaway
  • Pin-scaled misses 35 / 19 / 9.7 % at B/k = 1 / 2 / 4 against HiSparse’s 30 / 13.4 / 6.7 %: 1.2–1.5×, inside the 2× rule.
  • Same slope (1.07 against 1.08) and the same LRU-to-Bélády gap (1.58 against 1.63).
  • 65 % of picks repeat from step to step, against about 70 % on GLM-5.1.

Misses per layer per step, and bursts

The link sees misses per step, not a miss rate. Pin-scaled at B/k=2 has, per layer per step, a mean of 97 [94–104] misses of its 512 picks, a median of 88, a 90th percentile of 170 and a 99th percentile of 263. Pin-absolute has a mean of 205, against the 274 that HiSparse’s 13.4 % gives on the Pin (III.12 Three numbers and a deadline), and a 99th percentile of 679.

burst factormemory, caches & hardware
  • What it is. The 99th percentile of one layer’s misses per decode step, divided by their mean.
  • Example. Pin-scaled at B/k=2: 263 / 97 = 2.9 on average, 2.2 to 3.8 across layers. Pin-absolute: 3.5.
  • Easy to confuse with the spread across layers (below) and with the miss rate. The burst factor is about steps: one step in a hundred has at least 2.9 times the mean misses. A link sized for the mean is late at such a step unless the fetches are prefetched or spread over earlier steps.

Two settings move the mean:

  • The resident window. Keeping the last 128 positions resident raises the miss rate by 13–20 %. The window takes the easiest hits, recent positions that would have hit anyway, and the 512 picks move to farther positions, which repeat less. At V4’s ratio (W = 32 of a budget of 512 positions) the rise is 5–7 %.
  • Decode-like steps. Counted over assistant text and tool calls only (XI.2 › The dump), the miss rate is 1–9 % higher than over all steps.
Takeaway
  • Pin-scaled at B/k=2: 97 misses per layer per step on average, 263 at the 99th percentile; burst factor 2.9.
  • A resident window of 128 adds 13–20 % to the miss rate; decode-like steps add 1–9 %.

Layers

The napkins of Part VI use one miss rate for all 78 layers of the Pin. The replay reports each of the Bachelor’s 28 layers.

  • Layer 0 is local. It puts 39 % of its attention on the token itself and has no sink.
  • Even and odd layers differ. In layers 0 to 11, even layers miss about twice as often as odd ones: 24 % against 13 % at B/k=2.
  • The spread is wide. At B/k=2 the per-layer miss rates span 11–29 % between the 10th and 90th percentile of layers (9–33 % from the lowest to the highest). The Pin’s “274 misses per layer per step” is an average over layers that differ by a factor of about three.
  • Bursts differ by layer too. The burst factor of pin-scaled ranges from 2.2 to 3.8 between the 10th and 90th percentile of layers.
