XI.8

The shelf, replayed

Part VI assumed that a shelf of four dictionaries of 4096 positions catches 85 % of the misses, with 0.05 loads per step. On the Bachelor’s 27K-token context that shelf catches 94 %, because it spans 60 % of the context. Carried to the Pin’s 100K by a rule the data forced, it catches 36–54 %.

Part VI computed its shelf with assumed numbers: four dictionaries of 4096 positions per layer, catching fD​=0.85 of the misses, with nload​=0.05 loads per step (VI.9 The napkin for on fire). The replay measured both on the Bachelor, with the popular policy of XI.7 › Three ways to choose the shelf (P = 32), at the Pin’s budget share and B/k=2.

The δ-miss fraction against coverage

A shelf of nD​ dictionaries of stretches of b positions holds nD​b positions’ worth of dictionaries. On a short context that can be most of it.

shelf coveragethis project's own words
  • What it is. n_D * stretch_len / t: the positions the stretches on the shelf hold, over the context at step t.
  • Example. Four stretches of 4096 positions span 60 % of a 27K-token context on the Bachelor and 16 % of the Pin’s 100K.
  • Easy to confuse with the δ-miss fraction. If misses landed evenly, fD​ would equal the coverage; the shuffled twin of XI.7 › Heavy stretches against the shuffled twin gives exactly that value. A shelf is useful by the amount fD​ exceeds its coverage, read as the lift fD​ / twin.

Measured on the Bachelor (popular, P = 32, means over the whole sequence from t = 8192):

nD​ × b coverage fD​ twin lift net loads per step
1 × 1024 0.04 0.21 0.04 5.1 0.035
4 × 1024 0.15 0.57 [0.54–0.60] 0.16 3.6 0.089
8 × 1024 0.30 0.81 0.31 2.6 0.118
1 × 4096 0.15 0.46 0.15 3.0 0.017
4 × 4096 0.60 0.94 [0.93–0.96] 0.60 1.6 0.018

Part VI’s shelf, 4 × 4096, catches 0.94 of the misses here, above the assumed 0.85. It also spans 60 % of the context, and the twin, with no concentration at all, catches 0.60. At equal coverage, more and shorter stretches catch more: with nD​b=4096 positions on the shelf, one stretch catches 0.46 and sixteen stretches of 256 catch 0.64.

☞ Things to try
  1. Find the star and the Bachelor’s 4 × 4096 point. The Bachelor reaches 0.85 only with a shelf spanning over a third of its context.
  2. Switch the policy to touched. Every line drops toward the twin’s.
  3. Switch the Pin’s bars to b = 16 384. Four stretches catch 0.71–0.91 at a coverage of 0.65; eight would span the whole context.
Takeaway
  • On the Bachelor, 4 × 4096 catches 0.94 of the misses at 0.018 loads per step, but spans 60 % of the context (twin 0.60).
  • At small coverage the shelf catches 3–5× the twin’s share; at equal coverage, shorter stretches catch more.

The rule that overturned “the stretch length is absolute”

The design first kept b in absolute tokens, on the argument that what a dictionary is meant to catch, a file read or a tool output, has a length that does not grow with the context (XI.4 › Every setting scaled by what it serves). If that were so, fD​ at a fixed (nD​,b) would be the same in every time bin of a sequence.

It is not. At the Pin’s budget share, held fixed by choosing k=0.02t in each bin, fD​ at a fixed shelf falls as the context grows: for 4 dictionaries of 1024 positions, from 0.80 at about 10K to 0.48 at about 27K (derived, the budget interpolated to the share in each bin). Three rules were fitted to all bins, shelves and sequences, and their residuals compared (logit of fD​, derived):

rule fD​ depends on residual
absolute nD​ and b 0.42
share nD​ and b/t 0.15
fitted nD​ and b/t1.27 (exponent 0.93–1.44 over the five sequences) 0.12

Fitted on the bins before 20K and used to predict the later ones, the absolute rule is off by +0.13 in fD​ and the share rule by +0.03. Loads collapse in the same way, with an exponent of 1.24 [1.04–1.46]. Misses spread over more of the context as it grows, slightly faster than the context itself. A larger budget share spreads them as well: at a fixed context, fD​ falls with the budget as k−0.47 (1 × 1024), while the twin’s stays flat.

Takeaway
  • At a fixed share, fD​ at a fixed shelf falls as the context grows; it collapses on b/t1.27 (residual 0.12; absolute b 0.42; plain b/t 0.15).
  • A Pin stretch is carried by rescaling it, not by keeping its length; loads follow the same rule.

At the Pin, and the decision rule read

To carry a shelf to the Pin, read the Bachelor at the time bin 20K–24K (t ≈ 22.5K) with the stretch length rescaled to b×(22.5K/100K)0.93…1.44, the two ends of the fitted exponent, after shifting the bin’s share to the Pin’s 2.04 % along the budget sweep. A Pin stretch of 4096 positions then behaves like a Bachelor stretch of about 480 to 1020 positions. A reading is taken only where the rescaled stretch lands between two stretch lengths the Bachelor measured, 256 to 4096; nothing is extrapolated in the stretch length itself. Pin stretches of 1024 rescale to 120–255 positions, below the shortest measured stretch, and have no reading. Stretches of 2048 have one at the favourable end of the exponent only. Extrapolated, popular P = 32, fD​ with net loads per step:

nD​ b = 2048 (favourable end only) 4096 8192 16 384
1 0.12 (0.06) 0.11–0.20 (0.04–0.06) 0.19–0.30 (0.03–0.04) 0.29–0.43 (0.02–0.03)
4 0.37 (0.13) 0.36–0.54 (0.10–0.13) 0.52–0.73 (0.06–0.10) 0.71–0.91 (0.03–0.06)
8 0.57 (0.20) 0.56–0.76 (0.14–0.20) 0.75–0.93 (0.07–0.14) shelf spans the context
16 0.80 (0.28) 0.78–0.95 (0.15–0.29) shelf spans the context shelf spans the context

Where the shelf’s stretches span the whole 100K context, every stretch is on the shelf: fD​ = 1 and a dictionary loads only when a new stretch opens. Longer stretches catch more: four stretches of 16 384 positions catch 0.71–0.91 of the misses, at a coverage of 0.65.

The decision rule of X.9 What each outcome would mean was registered before the data: fD​≥0.8 at nD​≤8 with nload​≤0.1 reads “affordable”; 0.5≤fD​<0.8 “partly”; fD​<0.5 “spread”. On this replay it is read on the best cell with nD​≤8 and nload​≤0.1, and on the lift over the twin as well, because of coverage.

  1. The Bachelor, measured. “Affordable” only with a shelf spanning 73 % of the context (4 × 4096 in the bin 20K–24K: fD​ 0.90, 0.035 loads per step). Restricted to the Pin’s coverage (at most 0.33), “partly”: 0.52 at 4 × 1024, 0.098 loads per step.
  2. The Pin, extrapolated, stretches of 4096. “Partly” at best: 0.54 at 4 × 4096, with 0.10–0.13 loads per step, at the 0.1 limit. “Spread” at the other end of the exponent’s range. Part VI’s 0.85 at 4 × 4096 lies outside the range.
  3. The Pin, longer stretches. At the favourable end, 8 × 8192 (0.93, 0.07 loads per step) and 4 × 16 384 (0.91, 0.03) reach “affordable”; at the other end they read “partly” (0.75 and 0.71). Both shelves span 65 % of the context.

The rule was written about fD​ and loads alone. It says nothing about the GPU memory a shelf holds, and that is where the long-stretch shelves fail: four dictionaries of 16 384 positions at a dictionary share of one half hold about 33 000 slots’ worth per layer, seven resized hot caches (XI.9 › Spread over the punnet in the score, resident in serving, XI.10 › Stretch length against residency).
