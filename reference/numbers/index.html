XII.5

Grounded numbers and their sources

Every number this site leans on, in one place, with its grade and its source. The two frozen settings, the campaigns’ headline results, the pick traces of Part XI, the arithmetic of Parts III and VI, and the outside numbers from papers and from our own small checks.

A number without a source is a rumour. This page is the ledger: what each number is, which reference setting it belongs to, and how sure we are. Four grades apply to the campaigns’ numbers: verified (every cherry rebuilt bit for bit in a separate process, verified), quick (encoded bytes counted, no separate decode), code-length estimate (code-length estimate: a probability table’s code length on the data, no compressor built) and bound (a limit computed from a probability table fit to the data). Outside numbers are their measurement; the rest is arithmetic or an assumption, and says so.

The two settings

Both rigs are frozen in data/settings.yaml (III.11 Two reference settings: the Pin and the Bench). The pill in the top bar chooses one; in the setting selected now the LLM is GLM-5.1, one cherry of one token’s values at one layer is 640 B, 78 KV layers keep an orchard, and a sequence holds 100 384 tokens.

Both settings are assumptions about one machine each, and ratios travel between them better than absolute numbers: a ratio of bytes to bytes may cross from the Bench to the Pin with a flag; a miss rate cannot, because the Bench has none.

Takeaway
  • The Pin owns the miss side (picks, miss rates, step time); the Bench owns the byte side (shapes, formats, measured ratios).
  • Two Bench numbers are assumed: the link at 50 GB/s and 10 GB for orchards. The Pin’s 8-bit storage is assumed too.
