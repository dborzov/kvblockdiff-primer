VI.17

Two break-even conditions

A dictionary is a bet — paid for when it is loaded, repaid a little on every miss it serves. Two things turn it into a losing bet — loading too often, and decoding too slowly.

A dictionary on the GPU is a bet. You pay up front to bring it over the link. It pays you back a few hundred bytes at a time, on every miss in its block that arrives as a delta instead of a raw entry. The cost model of VI.15 The economics, written out already contains both sides of the bet; this page pulls out the two conditions under which it is a losing one.

Loads must be rare

Reminderδ-misses and the dictionary cache (IV.4)

A miss whose block has its dictionary on the GPU is a δ-miss and moves ρe bytes; otherwise it is a raw miss and moves e. The dictionary cache keeps nD​ dictionaries per layer; fD​ is the share of misses that are δ-misses.

One load of a dictionary of weight w moves we bytes — as much as w raw misses. In the reference setting at B=4096 a layer sees mk=0.134×2048≈274 misses per step. The first draft’s rule of thumb was therefore nload​w≪274.

That rule is generous. Loads do not compete with all miss traffic, only with what the deltas save:

entries’ worth loadednload​w​​<entries’ worth saved by δ-missesfD​(1−ρ)mk​​per layer, per step(VI.17·1)
SymbolsWhat’s in this equation (6)
nload​
dictionary loads [per step per layer] — How many dictionaries are loaded onto the GPU per decode step per layer. VI.15
w
dictionary weight [entries’ worth] — A dictionary’s size in entries’ worth: ∣D∣=we. IV.2
fD​
δ-miss fraction — Share of misses served by a delta (their dictionary was resident). IV.4
ρ
delta ratio — Delta size over entry size, ρ=∣δ∣/e≈bpe/8 (the rate also counts the amortized dictionary). Smaller is better. IV.2
m
miss rate — Fraction of requests (selections) not found in the cache; m(B) for a cache of size B. II.22
k
selections per step [entries] — How many entries the indexer selects per layer per decode step; 2048 in the reference setting. III.4

With the assumed fD​=0.85 and ρ=0.65, the right side is 0.85×0.35×274≈82 entries’ worth — about a third of the rule of thumb.

  • w=256: at most 82/256≈0.32 loads per step per layer. With 4 dictionaries resident, each must stay useful for at least about 13 steps just to break even, and for about 125 steps to keep loads below a tenth of the savings.
  • w=32: up to 2.6 loads per step per layer. Only a cache that thrashes could lose.
  • w=2 (loc): loads never matter.

Whether blocks stay busy that long is a property of the selections, not of the bytes: it is the lifetime of heavy blocks, which question C measures (VIII.7 Framework C: where do misses land?).

☞ Things to try
  1. Set fD​ to 1 and ρ to 0.3 — a very good dictionary. The 256-weight codebook now tolerates about 0.75 loads per step per layer, more than twice as many as before.
  2. Set ρ to 0.9. Every line starts just under 1, and even rare loads of a codebook push it over.
  3. Raise nD​ to 16 at the default settings. Each codebook must now live about 50 steps: more residents means more loads to pay for.
Running themeSide information at both ends

The receiver must actually have the almanac, and getting it there is not free. Block scope pays this price over and over; the break-even says how often it can afford to.

Takeaway
  • A load costs w raw misses. It must cost less than what δ-misses save: nload​w<fD​(1−ρ)mk, about 82 entries’ worth per layer per step with the assumed numbers.
  • Light dictionaries can be loaded freely; a 256-weight codebook must stay useful for tens of steps.

The decoder must keep up

Every δ-miss is decoded on the GPU before attention can use it, and the decoder must emit the raw entries at the rate the link would have carried them. Per GPU, that is N⋅fD​⋅mkNL​e/TPOT bytes per second. At N=110 with every miss a δ-miss:

110×30 ms13.7 MB​≈50 GB/s

— the speed of the link itself, as it must be. III.11 Three numbers and a deadline gave the rule of thumb: the decoder should run at least 10× faster than that, so that it eats no more than a tenth of the 0.38 ms each layer has per step. That means about 500 GB/s.

decoder output source
SplitZip, fixed-length, BF16 2.2 TB/s H200
SplitZip, fixed-length, FP8 E5M2 565 GB/s H200
DFloat11, Huffman 468 GB/s via SplitZip’s table
rANS-style decoders tens to a few hundred GB/s rough

Fixed-length decoders clear the bar by an order of magnitude; entropy decoders are borderline. That is why the fixed-length rate is the engineering number. Lever 2 in its full form is harsher still: it decodes every read, not only misses, which needs 110×2048×78×640 B/30 ms≈370 GB/s.

CarefulBulk numbers, scattered reads

Every throughput in the table was measured on long, contiguous streams. The miss path gathers about 274 scattered 640-byte deltas per layer per step, which is a different and probably slower access pattern. Nobody has measured a random-access delta decoder yet; it is part of the later engineering phase.

Takeaway
  • The miss path needs about 50 GB/s of decoder output per GPU at 110 requests; with 10× headroom, about 500 GB/s.
  • Fixed-length decoders (0.5–2 TB/s) have that headroom; entropy decoders are borderline; lever 2 needs about 370 GB/s and so fixed-length deltas.
