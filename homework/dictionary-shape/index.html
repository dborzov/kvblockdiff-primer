VI.4

The shape of every dictionary coder

A table of averages, a rotation, a codebook, a pointer to a neighbour: four tools that look nothing alike. Losslessly, they are one machine with three costs — and the one cost people forget is the tax paid on every single entry.

Here are four things a receiver might be handed before any entry arrives:

  • a short table: for each channel, a typical value and a typical spread;
  • a square matrix of numbers that turns the axes of the data;
  • a book of 256 prototype vectors;
  • nothing at all, but permission to look at the other entries of the same block.

They look like four different inventions. Losslessly, they are the same one.

Predict, subtract, code

Every lossless dictionary coder does three things to an entry, in this order:

  1. Predict the entry from the dictionary (and possibly from a little side information that comes with the entry).
  2. Subtract: take the exact integer residual, entry minus prediction, element by element. Exact integers make the round trip lossless.
  3. Code the residual. If the prediction was good, the residual is small, and small numbers have short codes.

That is predictive coding from II.10 Predict, then send the surprise, with the dictionary as the thing both ends know. Step through it on one toy entry.

Running themePredict, then store only the surprise

Here the theme is the whole skeleton of Part VI. The four dictionaries differ only in step 1 — how they predict and what the receiver must hold to repeat the prediction. Steps 2 and 3 never change.

Also on I.5 · II.1 · II.7 · II.10 · II.13 · II.15 · V.12 · VII.10
Takeaway
  • Predict from the dictionary, subtract exactly, code the residual: every lossless dictionary coder has this shape.
  • The dictionary types differ only in how they predict.

Three costs, one equation

The toy’s bill had three lines. Every dictionary coder pays the same three, and only these three:

per-entry taxthis project's own words
  • What: side information that must be stored with each entry, on top of its residual, so the receiver can repeat the prediction. Which centroid (log2​C bits), which sibling (log2​b bits), a flag.
  • Why it matters: it is paid again for every entry, and it travels with every miss. Spread over an entry’s d elements, we write it tax (bits per element).
  • Not to confuse with the dictionary’s own size: that is paid once per block and shared by all its entries.
ReminderDictionary weight and the block (IV.2)

A block is b consecutive entries. A dictionary of dictionary weight w is w entries’ worth of bytes, ∣D∣=we (IV.2 Dictionary, delta, block — precisely). At 8 bits per element an entry of d elements is e=d bytes, so the dictionary costs 8wd bits shared by bd elements: 8w/b bits per element. VI.14 The amortization rule turns this into the amortization rule.

For bell-shaped data the gain is the exchange rate of VI.3 The exchange rate, applied: gain=21​log2​1−R21​. In the toy: gain 1.00, tax 0.009, dictionary 0.125, so 0.87 bit saved per element.

The three costs live in different places of the system, and that is why they are kept apart:

cost paid stored on the link
residual H(r) per element in the entry’s delta with every miss
tax tax per entry in the entry’s delta with every miss
dictionary 8w/b per block once, as the block’s dictionary at load time

So the delta ratio is ρ=(H(r)+tax)/8, and the dictionary is the separate weight w of IV.2. Whether w is cheap depends on how often the dictionary must be loaded and how long it stays on the GPU (VI.15 The economics, written out).

Running themeEvery helper charges a tax

Here the theme gets its equation. Every helper a dictionary uses — an index, a pointer, a basis, a codebook — shows up as tax or as 8w/b. A dictionary type wins only where its gain exceeds both.

Also on II.6 · II.9 · II.17 · V.4 · V.10 · VI.7 · VI.15 · VII.5 · VII.13 · VIII.8 · IX.2
Takeaway
  • bpe = residual bits + per-entry tax + dictionary spread over the block.
  • Net saving against solo = gain − tax − 8w/b.
  • Residual and tax form the delta and ride on every miss; the dictionary is stored and loaded once per block.

Four ways to predict

Takeaway
  • loc and the full KLT predict without a per-entry tax; VQ and sib pay one on every entry.
  • Their weights range from about 2 entries’ worth (loc) to 2d (a full KLT basis).
