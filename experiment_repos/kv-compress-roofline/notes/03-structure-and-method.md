# Fact-check 03: structure of the V data and measurement method (campaign C2)

Checked 2026-10-31 against the repo `/attic/d/kv-compress-roofline` (read-only). Sources: `docs/concepts/terminology.md`, the supervisor's articles `research/agents/env/pages/articles/0{1,2,4,5,6,7}-*.md`, the researchers' iteration pages `research/agents/<id>/pages/iter-*.md`, `research/KNOWLEDGE.md`, `research/DEADENDS.md`, the ledgers `research/agents/<id>/ledger.jsonl`, and `research/agents/env/pages/{reads-recheck.json,retro-review.md}`.

Vocabulary (from `terminology.md`): an *entry* is what the receiver asks for (one head = 256 bytes, one layer = both heads = 512, one token = all 6 layers = 3072); the *entry split* says which; a *block* is 512 / 4096 / 16384 token positions; *MDL ratio* = (dictionary + all deltas) / raw, 8 x ratio = bits per V value; a *screen* (`s-`) is a tenth of the blocks on 2 sequences of `03_smoketest`, `st02n5-` the same on 5 sequences of `02_qwen`; a *same-window comparison* runs two coders on exactly the same V values; *paired difference* and *spread* are the difference per sequence and its SD over sequences; a *reference / tree* is an earlier entry that an entry is predicted from and the set of such links; *linear prediction* ("the map") guesses a V value from already-decoded values of the same entry; a *read budget* (`r137`, `r293`) caps how much dictionary one entry's decode may touch. Grades: **ledger, isolated** = every entry decoded alone in a separate process and found exact; **quick** = bit-exact in process, reads not counted; **block test / encoded bytes** = real encoder output, not verified by the isolated decoder; **bound / exact model** = a code-length calculation, no coder built. Almost everything here is on 2 sequences ("smoke-test grade" in the campaign's own words) unless a 5/10/20-sequence number is named.

Verdict scale: CONFIRMED (number and context found as stated), PARTLY (number found but context, grade or comparison differs), NOT FOUND, CONTRADICTED.

## 1. Entry splits: join the heads, then (barely) the layers; never adjacent tokens

**Claim (article 01; r24 iter-1-1..1-3, r12 iter-1-1, r9 iter-1-1..1-6).** On the same V values of 20 sequences, joining the two heads saves 0.0322 of MDL (spread 0.0009); joining all six layers saves another 0.0043 (spread 0.0014); `tok2`/`tok4` lose (0.726, 0.753 vs 0.677). The heads are "two views of one vector": same elephant line cosine 0.05 between heads but token-distance correlation up to 0.92. Shared bits between heads: 0.85 raw, 0.54 after a common reference, 0.21 with separate references. `regime` beats `triple` by 0.0015 on 7 of 7 sequences.

**Evidence.**

- *Head to layer −0.0322, layer to token −0.0043.* `research/agents/r24/pages/iter-1-3.md`: 20 windows (one 4096-token window per sequence of `02_qwen`), real encoders, "unverified encoded bytes with no ledger grade". Table: head (coder 102) 0.66036; layer (102) 0.62814, gap −0.03222, SD 0.00094; token (coder 94, three trees) 0.62382, gap −0.03654, SD 0.00202. Paired joins: head→layer −0.03216, spread 0.00086, 20 of 20; layer→token −0.00432, spread 0.00136, 20 of 20, 95 % [−0.00495, −0.00368]. Same numbers in `KNOWLEDGE.md [split/same-bytes]`. The article's 0.0322 / 0.0009 and 0.0043 / 0.0014 are these rounded. **CONFIRMED.** Strength: 20 sequences, but encoder bytes only, one window each, and the two coders differ (102 has path streams, 94 does not; r24 says joins beyond `layer` are understated by about 0.002).
- *`regime` beats `triple` by 0.0015, 7 of 7.* Same page: "triple to regime, 8 windows, 7 threads: −0.00148, spread 0.00065, 7 of 7". **CONFIRMED**, 7 sequences, encoded bytes.
- *`tok2`, `tok4` lose: 0.726, 0.753 vs 0.677.* Ledger `r12/ledger.jsonl` trial r12-t0004 (coder 62, `xmap_k: 0`, `streams: entry`, quick, isolated False): `s-tok2-b4k-u` 0.72642, `s-tok4-b4k-u` 0.75302, and in the same trial `s-layer-b4k-u` **0.68956**. - *Cosine 0.05, distance correlation up to 0.92.* `r7/pages/iter-1-1.md`, value-domain geometry on 36 blocks: "channel c of head 0 vs channel c of head 1, median cosine" 0.04–0.06 by layer; "correlation of pair distances, head 0 vs head 1" 0.25 (L3) … 0.92 (L19); "R² of copying head 0 onto head 1" −0.74 … −1.30. **CONFIRMED** (36 blocks, 2 sequences, no code lengths involved).

Note the r9 iter-1-3 correction: the no-reference floor between far cells is 0.12, not 0.19, once facts are counted once (chain rule); the article uses the uncorrected 0.19 for the "five layers apart" line.

- *Free model 0.677 / 0.641 / 0.618; coders take ~90 % and ~19 % of the gaps.* `r9/pages/iter-1-6.md` ("free model + 0.004 overhead": 0.677, 0.641, 0.618); r24 iter-1-2: 0.0322/0.036 ≈ 90 %, 0.0043/0.023 ≈ 19 %. **CONFIRMED** (unpaired: the free rows are 4 blocks of `03`, the coder gaps are 20 windows of `02`).
- *Price of joining at 512 tokens (0.69832 / 0.69451 / 0.69722 / 0.70097) and "stored rule cost 0.0184 of a 0.0265 gap".* `KNOWLEDGE [token/b]`, `[token/gap]`; `r18/pages/iter-1-1.md` title "0.0184 of 0.0265", 4 blocks. **CONFIRMED** at block-test grade (r18 pages not read in full).

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED, with one PARTLY** (the tok2/tok4 comparison mixes two settings of coder 62). **Strength:** the two headline gaps are 20 sequences but unverified encoder bytes, one window each; the geometric and information numbers are 2 sequences and 4–36 blocks, mostly bounds. The only ledger-grade, isolated-verified split numbers on 5 sequences are 102 at `st02n5-layer` 0.63848 and at `st02n5-head` 0.67015 (r24-t0002), which are different block sets and may not be subtracted.

**What this means (plain English).** The model computes two 256-number "head" vectors for each token at each layer. Number 32 of head 0 has nothing to do with number 32 of head 1 (cosine 0.05), so you cannot copy one onto the other. But if two tokens are far apart in head 0 they are far apart in head 1 too: the heads are two different camera angles on the same hidden object. A compressor that codes both heads together can describe head 1 as a fixed linear picture of head 0, and that alone is worth about 4 % of the file. Layers are more independent of one another (each is a different stage of the computation), so bundling them adds only about half a percent more. Bundling neighbouring tokens into one entry hurts, because the trick that works best, pointing at an earlier similar token, needs one pointer per token, and a pair of tokens rarely has a good earlier pair to point at.

## 2. The two kinds of structure: repeats (tree) and geometry (linear prediction)

**Claim (article 02).** Tree and linear-prediction gains overlap by only 0.024–0.042; shuffling tokens within blocks destroys the tree gain but leaves the prediction gain unchanged; a rule fit on another sequence recovers 91–96 % of the gain; the shape correlates 0.9995 between datasets; "sparsity was a price" (dense-with-rounding beat sparse); storing the rule costs 0.013–0.021 of MDL at 4k; head 1 is largely an image of head 0 (L19: 4,103 terms inside head 0 vs 29,835 from head 0 into head 1).

Map alone beats tree at L7 (0.091 vs 0.087) and L11 (0.077 vs 0.064). **CONFIRMED**; strength: six single blocks of one sequence.
- *Shuffle test.* `r12/pages/iter-1-2.md` and `iter-1-3.md`, real encoders on shuffled `03` dev blocks (each block a random sample of a layer's tokens, both heads of a token kept together): base head coder 0.6907 → 0.7520 (destroys about 70 % of the exemplar gain over solo); the shared-reference gain −0.0030 → −0.0031 and the cross-head map −0.0098 → −0.0093 (14 windows); the sparse triangular map (coder 80) −0.0337 → −0.0344 and the forward-selected cross-head map (74) −0.0232 → −0.0210, with the base coder 0.711 → 0.773 (8 windows). - *91–96 % recovered by a rule fit elsewhere.* `r7/pages/iter-1-2.md`: map fit on 6 blocks of the other sequence, tested on one block per layer, gross gain, exact model: shares 91, 94, 94, 91, 96, 91 %. **CONFIRMED** (one test block per layer).
- *0.9995 between datasets.* `r9/pages/iter-1-3.md`: the 66-pair Gaussian shared-information matrix on 10 sequences of `02_qwen` vs the `03` dev pair, correlation 0.9995, per-sequence SD 2–3 %. **CONFIRMED**, 10 sequences (Gaussian estimate, not code lengths).
- *Another sequence's term pattern refit here costs +0.0095 at L11; 121 at 0.61239 beats the free dense rule 0.6132 on four of six layers.* `r22/pages/iter-1-2.md` (one block per layer, sequence 0000; `KNOWLEDGE [graph/stable]`, `[limit/free]`). **CONFIRMED**, six single blocks.
- *Rank 64 saved 0.058 vs 0.110 for 32 terms; with ≥ 32 parts the encoder chose rank 0 on all 18 blocks.* `KNOWLEDGE [layers/who]` (bound, 2 blocks, r8); `r14/pages/iter-1-2.md`: "at 32 parts it is 0 on all 18 blocks, at every layer" (real coders, 18 blocks, 2 sequences). **CONFIRMED.**

- *Head 1 is an image of head 0: 4,103 / 29,835.* `r22/pages/iter-1-2.md`: "In natural order at L19, head 0 keeps only 4,103 terms among itself … Head 1 takes 29,835 terms from head 0, and head 0 explains 73 % of its residual variance"; natural order beats Chow-Liu order by −0.0045 at L19. `KNOWLEDGE [graph/heads]` adds the within-head-1 count 10,587 and L7/L3 figures. **CONFIRMED**, one block per layer.
- *Storage cost 0.013–0.021 of MDL at 4k; r23's 0.6407 → 0.6326.* `r22/pages/iter-1-1.md`: "The map costs 0.0129 to 0.0208 of MDL" (six blocks). `r23/pages/iter-1-2.md`: 102 0.6407, weights chain + smaller paid map 0.6326, Δ −0.0081, 12 of 12 blocks, exact model, "I did not build or validate a coder, and this one could not be legal today". **CONFIRMED** (as bounds).
- *The ladder (71 → 121) and 121 vs 102 on 5 sequences −0.0088, spread ~0.0001, reads 120 vs 103.* Ledgers: r7-t0001 (71) 0.67746, r7-t0003 (74) 0.66641, r11-t0001 (80) 0.65620, r11-t0003 (89) 0.64704, r11-t0002 (102) 0.64494, r22-t0001 (111) 0.64352, r22-t0003 (114) 0.64167, r22-t0005 (115) 0.64003, r20-t0001 (126) 0.64074, env-t0007 (121) 0.63639, all isolated-verified on `s-layer-b4k-r293`; env-t0096 121 `st02n5-layer` 0.62970 vs r24-t0002 102 0.63848; per-sequence differences −0.00883, −0.00887, −0.00861, −0.00874, −0.00886 (SD ≈ 0.0001); reads 120.4 vs 102.9. **CONFIRMED**, ledger grade, 5 sequences.

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED.** **Strength:** the ladder and the 121-vs-102 step are isolated-verified on 5 sequences. The mechanism statements (overlap, shuffle, portability, sparsity, head-1-as-image) rest on single blocks or 8–41 windows of the two `03` sequences; the one 10-sequence number is a Gaussian estimate.

**What this means (plain English).** Two unrelated things make V compressible. First, text repeats: the same word in the same context produces nearly the same V vector, so a block can store one copy and let later tokens point at it (the "tree"). Shuffle the tokens of a block and these nearby repeats vanish, so the tree gain collapses. Second, the model's weights force every V vector to lie near a fixed, roughly 100-dimensional tilted sheet inside the 512-dimensional space, so once part of a vector is decoded, the rest can be guessed as a weighted sum. That sheet is a property of the network, not of the text: a rule learned on one conversation works on another (91–96 %), and shuffling does nothing to it. The expensive part of the second trick is writing down the weighted-sum rule in every block (about 1.5–2 % of the file); a rule shared across all blocks would be nearly free, but the campaign's rules did not yet allow a shared table.

## 3. Codebooks and transforms: a stored codeword never beats an earlier entry

**Claim (article 04; r25, r26, r10, r11).** RQ gains 0.0029 on prediction alone (14/14 segments), PQ 0.0017, both ~0.05 behind a tree; stacked on tree + prediction the encoder took no code on 72 blocks; DCT / Hadamard / random rotation lose by +0.28 to +0.32 bit/value; rotating-then-rounding costs ~0.25 bit/value; coder 129 (no tree) 0.7348 at `head` … 0.6622 at `token`; anchors from 64 up to the whole tree always improve (L3 0.6858 → 0.5737 etc.); "an exemplar pays for itself, a centroid does not; a pointer costs ~12 bits"; coverage: 62 techniques, 21 real coder, 18 bound only, 16 untried, 7 n/a.

- *Coverage 62 = 21 + 18 + 16 + 7.* `r25/pages/iter-1-1.md` table: counted 62 rows; statuses begin "real" 21, "bound" 18, "not tried" 16, "n/a" 7. `fig-04-coverage.svg` labels agree. **CONFIRMED.** The article's note that r26 then moved "about six" techniques into the real-coder bar is a statement of the article, not re-counted anywhere.
- *"Contiguous and random chunks cost the same, 0.6981 against 0.6982; the gain falls as 1/m".* `KNOWLEDGE [quant/no-order]` cites r10 h1a, h1b. `r10/pages/iter-2-2.md` gives "half a head 0.6981" in the *reference-unit* (mosaic: one exemplar tree per chunk) table and says the Gaussian gain inside a head falls roughly as 1/m under contiguous, random or clustered partitions (0.263 → 0.13, 0.07, 0.04); `DEADENDS` (r10): "Random and contiguous chunks identical". The 0.6982 figure itself appears only in KNOWLEDGE. **PARTLY**: the numbers come from a chunked-reference bound, not from a PQ codebook; the conclusion (no good way to cut a head into independent pieces) is supported by r10's 1/m result and by r26's PQ-vs-RQ 40 % loss.
- *Cutting a word across the two heads gives up ~40 % of RQ's gain; L3 block mean delta 360 → 348 bytes, top tenth carries 42 %; K = 16, 64, 1024 won no block.* `r26/pages/iter-1-3.md`. **CONFIRMED**, 12 blocks per layer / one block for delta sizes.

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED, with one PARTLY** (the 0.6981/0.6982 pair). **Strength:** the quantiser family is 2 sequences, one isolated-verified row (RQ no-tree) and ten `--quick` rows; the transform results are exact-model bounds on two heads, never a coder; the anchor curve is three single blocks; 16k blocks and token entries have no graded quantiser row (r26-t0012 is quick and unfinished). The article states these limits itself.

**What this means (plain English).** A codebook compressor stores a short list of "typical" vectors and sends, for each entry, the number of the closest one plus the small correction. The catch is that every stored typical vector costs bytes and helps only others. An earlier real entry of the block has to be stored anyway, so using it as the "typical vector" costs nothing but a pointer of about 7–12 bits, and it is a better match because text really repeats. That is why every stored-codebook variant (VQ, PQ, OPQ, RQ, PCA, binary codes) lost to pointing at an earlier entry, and why a codebook added on top of the pointer-plus-prediction coder was never chosen. Fixed rotations such as the DCT fail for a second reason: they assume neighbouring positions in the vector are related, and in V position 17 has no relation to position 18; rotating also forces a rounding step that costs about a quarter of a bit per value, which prediction avoids by never rounding.

## 4. The time axis and FLAC: prediction is right, time is the wrong axis

**Claim (article 05; r16, r17, r20, r21).** An order-8 filter along tokens saves up to 0.33 bit alone but ≤ 0.06 after a reference; adjacent-token share of references 16–30 % in L7/11/15 vs 7–12 % in L3/19/23; neural network 6.435 → 5.804 → 5.636 bits at L7 with no reference, ~nothing with one; coder 126 0.64074 vs 0.64494 on 72/72 blocks; at 512 tokens a learned-in-decoder rule beats a stored one (0.6902 vs 0.6998).

**Evidence.**

- *Order-8 filter: up to 0.33 alone, ≤ 0.06 after a reference, not a code.* `r16/pages/iter-1-1.md` table (4 mid-thread blocks per series, 2 sequences, exact model): LPC order 8 alone +0.329 (L7 h0), +0.331 (L11 h0); after the reference at most +0.063 (L7 h1); shuffled control +0.001; "the 'LPC after the reference' column is not a code that can exist" (cycle through the tree). **CONFIRMED**, bound.

The article's "16 to 30 % adjacent … about 37 to 47 % within 8 … 7 to 12 %" splices r16's adjacent share with r6's within-8 share; r16's own within-8 range (25–52 %) is wider. **PARTLY**: each number exists, from two different samples and tree builds; the article does not say so.
- *FLAC table.* Fixed polynomials −0.41 to +0.13 (r16 iter-1-1 ✓); order above 1 with a reference "under 0.005" (DEADENDS r16 h6: "order 2 adds under 0.005"; r16 iter-1-2 says at most 0.003 ✓); per-segment widths ≤ +0.009 (DEADENDS ✓; page says ≤ +0.010); stereo nothing (✓); blend −0.0016 / −0.0003 (r16 iter-1-2, iter-1-3, 7 and 2–3 blocks per layer, in-process ✓; the graded blend r16-t0001 vs r16-t0002 is −0.00033, verdict noise); serial coding 0.02–0.05 bit better with unbounded reads, worse under any cap (`KNOWLEDGE [family/blend]`, r16 iter-1-2 ✓). **CONFIRMED.**

- *Neural network 6.435 → 5.804 → 5.636; ~nothing with a reference; 45 % control.* `r21/pages/iter-1-1.md`: "One run, L7, no exemplar and no block … fit on 100,000 tokens of one thread, tested on the other. Solo 6.435 bit/element, ridge 5.804, net 5.636"; after 102's reference and map the net adds −0.0020 … −0.0100 bit/value, trees −0.0038 … −0.0224; positive control recovers 45 %, "read as at most about 0.02"; fit on half a 4k block the net loses at three layers of four. **CONFIRMED**; exact model, 2 sequences, the no-reference figure is one layer in one direction.
- *Coder 126: 0.64074 vs 0.64494, 72 of 72 blocks; two thirds not holdable by a shared rule.* Ledger r20-t0001 `s-layer-b4k-r293` 0.64074, isolated True; r11-t0002 (102) 0.64494, isolated True; `r20/pages/iter-1-2.md`: Δ −0.00420, SD 0.00059 per block, 72 of 72, 14 of 14 segments; fixed coefficient per block 0.010–0.022 bit/value vs the entry's own 0.031–0.044. Reads re-measured in env-t0011 for `s-head` (68 / 123 / 171). **CONFIRMED**, ledger grade, 2 sequences.
- *At 512 tokens learned beats stored: 0.6902 vs 0.6998.* `r17/pages/iter-1-2.md`: layer, b = 512, 48 blocks, real bytes in process, paired on the same blocks: exemplar alone 0.71174, paid sparse rows (89) 0.69981, learned (96) 0.69016; at 4k the paid rows lead (0.64999 vs 0.65317). Ledger r17-t0002 (106, the fast decoder of the same model) `s-layer-b512-r293` 0.69602 isolated; r21-t0001 (113, stack) 0.68938 isolated; 455 stored terms / 0.0036 of raw vs 0.041 at 4k in `r21/pages/iter-1-2.md`. **CONFIRMED**; the 0.6902 vs 0.6998 pair is a 48-block in-process test, the graded numbers are 0.69602 and 0.68938.

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED, with one PARTLY** (the reference-locality percentages are stitched from two measurements). **Strength:** 126 and the b = 512 coders are isolated-verified on 2 sequences; everything about time filters and networks is exact-model bounds on 1–4 blocks; the blend coder's graded difference is below noise.

**What this means (plain English).** FLAC compresses sound by guessing each sample from the few before it. Tokens look like time, and V really is smooth along tokens in the middle layers (a single value correlates 0.48 with its successor), so a filter along tokens does save about a third of a bit per value on its own. But the pointer-to-a-similar-earlier-token trick already captures this: in the middle layers it very often points at the adjacent token, so after the pointer the filter has almost nothing left (≤ 0.06 bit, and even that cannot be decoded without reading the whole past). The same mathematics works far better across the 512 positions of one entry, because the network's geometry makes those positions predict one another. A neural network helps only when there is no pointer; the pointer is itself a nonlinear predictor (nearest neighbour) and removes the curvature. The one thing an entry can learn about itself while decoding is how much of its parent's private quirk to undo (coder 126), and at tiny blocks it is cheaper to let the decoder learn the prediction rule from the entries it has already decoded than to ship the rule.

## 5. Random access and block size

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED.** **Strength:** the reads and the three block-size scores are the best-graded numbers in the whole campaign (isolated, every entry decoded cold). The scaling statements (per-doubling gains, the whole-sequence pool) are one window of one sequence on 2–3 layers, and the pool figure is a bound that allows cycles and free pointers. The retro-review records that an earlier draft of the budget table was wrong and that `env-t0008` is a false `lossy` and `env-t0014…t0087` are 74 duplicate rows.

**What this means (plain English).** The receiver asks for one entry at a time, so a coder is not allowed to require decoding the whole block to answer. The fear was that this rule would cost a lot. It did not: a tree coder only has to walk from the requested entry up its chain of parents to the root, which touches a tiny fraction of the block, once the bytes are laid out along those chains. The rule bites only at the very tightest budget and only through the few unlucky entries at the end of long chains (the worst entry reads up to 3.7 times the average). Block size is the opposite kind of knob: making blocks bigger costs nothing to ask for, and it keeps paying, because a bigger window of text contains more repeats to point at; the prediction rule, by contrast, is a fixed cost that is paid off by about 32k tokens. Middle layers build trees that are chains along the text (the next token looks like this one); the first and last layers build hubs where many far-apart repeats of one token hang off a single copy.

## 6. Noise and the trap: pairing is the whole trick

**Claim (article 07; r12 iter-1-1, r24 iter-1-3).** Between-sequence SD ~0.012 vs paired SD 0.0001–0.001; the escalation table (102 vs 89: −0.00210 → −0.00212, spread 0.00006, etc.); the trap: layer screen 0.6449 vs token screen 0.6461, but same-window token better by 0.0043 on 20/20, the token screen's blocks 0.0046 harder; C1's fixed 0.002 limit was 5–30× the noise; one number per token window explains 97 % of block variance and correlates −0.91 with repeated-token share; 74–99 % of a coder's gain variance is between layers.

**Evidence.**

- *Process incidents (crash, saturation, status loop, lossy time-outs).* `retro-review.md` items 11–12 and the "Mistakes" paragraph record the time-out → `unverified` fix (r22-t0002 and r19-t0002 were false `lossy`; re-runs r22-t0005 0.64003 and r19-t0004 0.62105 passed). **CONFIRMED** for the parts checked; the commit-count figures were not re-counted.

**Overall verdict: CONFIRMED, with one PARTLY** (the 74 % lower bound has no visible receipt). **Strength:** the escalation table is the best-supported method result in the campaign: four coder pairs, isolated-verified, 5 sequences, and the 2-sequence screen predicted each within 0.001. The hardness analysis is 2 sequences / 110 windows. The article's own caveats stand: 115 and 126 have 2 sequences behind them; nothing but one 5-sequence track was ever run in full; all sequences are the same kind of text (coding-agent prefills).

**What this means (plain English).** Different conversations compress very differently (the score moves by ±0.012 from one to the next), mostly because some texts repeat themselves more: a single "how repetitive is this window" number explains 97 % of the block-to-block variation and tracks the share of repeated tokens (r = −0.91). That looks like hopeless noise if you compare raw scores. But two coders run on the *same* blocks rise and fall together, so their *difference* is steady to about 0.0001, a hundred times tighter. Hence two sequences were enough to rank refinements of one mechanism, and every such ranking survived five sequences. The one trap was comparing scores from two different subsets of blocks: the token-entry screen happened to contain slightly harder text than the layer-entry screen (+0.0046), which made the token split look worse when on identical data it is better. The campaign's fix was to always compare on the same V values and to judge a difference against its own spread rather than against a fixed threshold.
