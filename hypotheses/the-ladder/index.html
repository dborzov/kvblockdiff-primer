VII.9

The cat-sequence ladder

Every proposal has an argument that made it feel obvious. This one has a story about cats, three rungs of a ladder, and a jump at the end. Here it is, told the way it was first told, sorted into what is right, what leaks, and what is a trap.

Picture two very long sequences with the same LLM. In cat_seq it writes an overview of cat breeds: 100K tokens on the Maine Coon, then 10K on the Siamese, and so on. In dog_seq it does the same for dogs. Pick one cherry, call it R, from the middle of the Maine Coon part, and ask: how surprising is R?

Three rungs

The first draft answered with three rungs, each comparing R with a narrower crowd.

  1. Against everything the LLM could ever be asked about, cats, helicopters, Python, tax law: R is one message out of an enormous set. Very surprising.
  2. Against the cherries of cat_seq. The sequence is known to be about cats. The set of plausible messages is much smaller. Less surprising.
  3. Against the cherries of the surrounding 100K tokens, all on the Maine Coon. Smaller still. Least surprising of all.
the cat-sequence ladder“the Ladder”this project's own words
  • What it is. The intuitive argument for this project: a cherry is less surprising against its own sequence than against everything, and least surprising against its own neighbourhood, so dictionaries fitted on narrower crowds should code it in fewer bits.
  • Mnemonic. A cat climbing down a ladder, one rung per narrower crowd: everything, the sequence, the stretch.
  • Verdict, in advance. Right in direction, leaky in its evidence, harsh in its exchange rate: this page and the next two.

The story is good because it is concrete. And each rung is something one could build: rung 1 is one dictionary for every sequence, fitted once on a large sample of text; rung 2 is one dictionary per sequence, fitted on its own orchard; rung 3 is one dictionary per stretch. Those are the three scopes of a dictionary, global, sequence and stretch, of IV.10 Who pays for the dictionary: scope.

ReminderSurprise is measured in bits

The surprisal of an outcome with probability p is −log2​p bits; the entropy H is its average, the fewest bits per symbol any lossless code can average (II.3 Surprise, II.4 Entropy: the average surprise). “Less surprising” means “codable in fewer bits”.

Takeaway
  • Three rungs: a cherry against everything, against its sequence, against its stretch.
  • Each rung is a dictionary scope: global, sequence, stretch.

What it gets right, and the jump

Before the criticism, credit. The ladder says, in symbols,

against everythingH(E)​​≥against its sequenceH(E∣sequence)​​≥against its neighbourhoodH(E∣stretch)​​,(VII.9·1)
SymbolsWhat’s in this equation (2)
H
entropy [bits] — Average surprisal: the fewest bits per symbol any lossless code can average. II.4
E
a cherry as a vector — One cherry flattened to a vector of d elements: Ei​ is cherry i of a punnet. IV.5

and this is not a hypothesis. Conditioning never increases entropy on average (VII.1 Bits are the unit); and knowing the stretch also tells you the sequence, so the bottom rung knows at least as much as the middle one. What the theorem does not say is how big the steps are. They can be zero.

☞ Things to try
  1. Set both “differ by” sliders to 0. All three rungs are equal: the ladder is flat, and no dictionary at any scope saves a bit.
  2. Raise only the sequences’ spread. The top step grows, the bottom step stays at zero: sequence-scope dictionaries would help, stretch-scope ones would not.
  3. Raise the noise inside a stretch. Every step shrinks: noise that no rung can explain dilutes them all.

With the defaults the toy’s rungs read 3.17, 2.60 and 2.43 bits: steps of 0.57 and 0.17 bit. The theorem guarantees the order; the sliders set the gaps; only data gives the sizes. Two more things the argument gets right: its unit is bits, which convert to bytes on the link; and sameness of subject is real, since cherries about one passage do have typical values that sit closer together, which is exactly what the lightest dictionary, a location per furrow, captures (V.3 Furrow statistics: the Anchor).

Then came the step that made the argument feel finished: “HiSparse’s high hit rate shows that this ladder is steep at the bottom rung, so a stretch dictionary should work.” It points at a real number, 87 % of picks hitting the hot cache, and attaches it to the rung we care about most. There are three different things in it.

piece of the argument verdict page
narrower crowds can only help right: a theorem this page
surprise is measured in bits right: our unit VII.1 Bits are the unit
cherries of one subject share something right: a location per furrow captures it V.3 Furrow statistics: the Anchor
HiSparse’s hit rate shows the bottom rung is steep a leak: the hit rate is about queries VII.10 The leak: hit rate is about queries
a steep rung means a big saving a trap: the exchange rate is harsh VII.11 The trap: the exchange rate is harsh
IntuitionWhy keep a story we are about to take apart?

Because it is not taken apart, it is sorted. The parts that are right became the measurement plan: three rungs, three scopes, three numbers in bits. The parts that are wrong say which evidence not to trust and how big a result to expect.

Takeaway
  • Direction: a theorem. Unit: bits. Subject: real. Size: not guaranteed, can be zero.
  • The jump borrows a which-number for a what-claim; the next two pages price it.

Verdict

The rungs, measured so far, on the values of Qwen3.5-2B, FP8, v[t, l, :, :] cherries:

rung dictionary scope reading grade
bottom: the stretch stretch 4096 solo 0.778 (6.22 bits per element) to the best verified compressor 0.636 (5.09 bits): 1.14 bits per element won by the punnet’s own dictionary verified, every cherry, on the two-sequence screen; 0.630 on five sequences (VIII.12 The scorecard, VIII.11 Signal or noise)
middle: the sequence stretch 16 384, and a whole sequence 0.618 at 16 384 positions; a pool over the whole sequence is a bound 0.26 to 0.45 bit per element below that; the punnet floors sit near 0.60 and 0.57 verified at 16K; the whole sequence is an optimistic bound (VIII.15 Shared across the stretch, in bits)
top: everything global, a calibration corpus unmeasured: gseq​, what a sequence-scope dictionary saves over a global one, has never been run; the one hint is that the linear rule across elements carries over between sequences at 91 to 96 % X.4 Next in the harness

The ladder does go down, and the bottom rung alone bought more than the variance budget of VII.11 The trap: the exchange rate is harsh predicted for it. The reason is the shape of the structure, echoes and the stereo map, and that is where the next two pages end up.

SourceReceipts

Takeaway
  • Stretch rung: 1.14 bits per element, verified. Sequence rung: a bound so far. Global rung: unmeasured.
  • The order held; the sizes were not what the toy budget expected, in the project’s favour.
