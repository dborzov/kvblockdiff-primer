VII.5

Stereo similarity (head axis)

Hold a stereo pair of photographs. Pixel for pixel the two pictures disagree; object for object they agree completely. The two KV heads of a layer are that pair. Element for element they share a cosine of 0.05, yet joining them into one cherry was the strongest structure the campaigns measured.

In the decode_step listing of III.2 Attention: looking back one token’s values at one layer are made in one line, val = (x @ W.v[layer_id]).reshape(n_kv_heads, head_dim): one hidden state, one matrix, then cut into n_kv_heads pieces. On the Bench that is two pieces of 256, v[t, l, 0, :] and v[t, l, 1, :]. They come from the same x. Do they share anything a compressor can use?

The first campaign looked at the obvious statistic and said no. The second looked at the bits and found the strongest structure in the orchard.

Two views of one vector

stereo similarity“the Stereo Pair”this project's own words
  • What it is. The two KV heads of a layer at one token are two views of one vector: v[t, l, 0, :] and v[t, l, 1, :] are two linear pictures of the same hidden state, so each predicts the other through a fixed linear map, though element for element they look unrelated. A hypothesis about the head_id axis.
  • Mnemonic. A stereo pair: pixel for pixel different, object for object the same scene.
  • Status. Measured: joining the heads into one cherry saves 0.0322 of ratio, with a bound of 0.85 bit per element in common. The strongest structure the campaigns found.

Three measured facts, values of Qwen3.5-2B, 36 punnets of two sequences, that look like a contradiction:

statistic reading
cosine between element c of head 0 and element c of head 1, median over elements 0.04 to 0.06 at every KV layer
[[r-squared R²]] of copying head 0 onto head 1 as a prediction
correlation of token-to-token distances: are two tokens far apart in head 0 also far apart in head 1? 0.25 at KV layer 0, rising to 0.92 at KV layer 4

Element 32 of head 0 has nothing to do with element 32 of head 1. But the geometry agrees: whatever makes two tokens distant in one head makes them distant in the other. That is what two projections of one vector look like. A toy makes it exact:

It prints cosine per element 0.01 R² of copying -0.98 distance correlation 0.80 R² of the map 0.91. Same three symptoms as the data, and a cure: a map from all of head 0 to all of head 1 explains 91 % of head 1, worth 21​log2​1−0.911​≈1.7 bits per element at the exchange rate of II.12 The exchange rate: variance explained → bits. The toy’s numbers are the toy’s; the shape is the point.

The real map is not a toy’s. At KV layer 4 the campaign’s linear prediction across elements (V.7 Linear prediction across elements), offered every element of a token as a possible term, kept 4 103 terms inside head 0 and 29 835 from head 0 into head 1: head 0 explains 73 % of head 1’s residual variance there, and a map fit on another sequence recovers 91 to 96 % of the gain. The link between the heads is a property of W.v[layer_id], not of the text.

Takeaway
  • Element for element the heads are strangers (cosine 0.05, copying worse than the mean); token for token they agree (distances correlate up to 0.92).
  • Two projections of one hidden state behave exactly so; a linear map, not a copy, is what connects them.

In bits

Why does a cosine near zero hide bits? Because cosine compares element c with element c, one furrow against one furrow, and the information runs across furrows: element 57 of head 1 is a weighted sum of many elements of head 0 (VII.1 Bits are the unit). A lossless compressor that may see head 0 while coding head 1 can use that sum; a cosine never looks for it.

How a scenario measures it (IV.11 Why we vary the scenario): the same slice, two cherry shapes, the same compressor, the same elements. With cherry(t) = v[t, l, h, :] (256 elements) the receiver may ask for head 1 without head 0, so head 1 must be coded alone. With v[t, l, :, :] the compressor codes head 0 first and may predict head 1 from it. A wider cherry can always run the narrower compressor inside, so the gap is a lower bound on what the heads share.

what was measured reading grade
ratio with v[t, l, h, :] cherries, compressor 102, 20 windows 0.6604 encoded bytes, one window per sequence
ratio with v[t, l, :, :] cherries, same compressor, same elements 0.6281 encoded bytes
the join saves 0.0322 ± 0.0009 of ratio, 20 of 20 sequences, about 37 times its spread paired, 20 sequences
bits the heads share, raw 0.85 per element code length, 4 punnets
after one reference cherry serves both heads 0.54 code length, 4 punnets
after each head gets its own reference cherry 0.21 code length, 4 punnets
of head 1’s plateau after its reference, visible in head 0 at KV layers 1, 2, 4 66 to 70 % code length

The three bound numbers tell the story of the join. Raw, the heads share 0.85 bit per element. A reference cherry, an earlier token that both heads are predicted from, explains part of what they had in common, leaving 0.54. Give each head its own reference and only 0.21 remains, because two different earlier tokens no longer line up. So the natural unit is one reference per token per layer, serving both heads, and a v[t, l, :, :] cherry is where that unit lives.

Running themeBits are the unit

The warning of the old draft was that a high cosine can hide a useless neighbour. Here it cut the other way: a cosine near zero hid 0.85 bit per element. Only the bits settled it.

What it implies

For cherry shapes: join the heads, always. A cherry of one head throws away the cheapest bits in the orchard; whether to join layers or tokens as well is a separate and much weaker question (VII.4 Upstairs similarity (layer axis), VII.3 Fallen-apple similarity (token axis)). For serving designs (VI.13 Which application, which scenario): a pick in DSA or GLM-5.1 names a position for a layer, and attention at that layer reads every KV head of that position, so both views travel together and the saving comes free inside the v[t, l, :, :] cherry. A design whose picks covered one head at a time would pay for it, in a dictionary that carries the other head’s picture or in raw bytes. On the Pin there is no second head: GLM-5.1 keeps one MLA latent per token per layer, and what that latent shares with itself across its 640 elements is the same question asked of one vector (V.7 Linear prediction across elements).

Takeaway
  • Joining the heads: 0.0322 ± 0.0009 of ratio on 20 of 20 sequences, 0.26 bit per element of the 0.85 the bound allows.
  • One reference per token per layer serves both heads (0.54 bit kept in common); a cherry holds both heads, and picks per layer get the saving free.

Verdict

The strongest structure measured, and the one the old draft had declined to pursue. Values of Qwen3.5-2B, FP8, stretch 4096: 0.0322 ± 0.0009 of ratio on 20 of 20 sequences for the join, a bound of 0.85 bit per element shared raw, 0.54 after one reference, 0.21 after two, 66 to 70 % of head 1’s plateau visible in head 0, and 91 to 96 % of the map’s gain recoverable from a map fit on another sequence (VIII.6 Cutting the orchard: cherry shapes compared, VIII.5 Two kinds of structure).

Grades: the 20-sequence join is encoded bytes on one window per sequence, not a verified ledger row; the bounds are four punnets of two sequences; the only verified split scores on five sequences (0.6385 for v[t, l, :, :], 0.6702 for v[t, l, h, :]) read different punnets and may not be subtracted. Unmeasured: the same question on keys, whose two heads were coded jointly for one night only (VII.12 Keys before RoPE; keys and values apart), and stereo across layers, a map from one layer’s cherry into another’s (X.4 Next in the harness).

SourceReceipts

Cosine, copying R² and distance correlation: r7 iter-1-1 (36 blocks). Same-window join: r24 iter-1-3 (20 windows of target 02). Shared bits 0.85 / 0.54 / 0.21: r9 iter-1-2 (4 blocks, exact code lengths). Terms inside and across heads at L19, 73 %: r22 iter-1-2. Map fit elsewhere 91–96 %: r7 iter-1-2. Plateau 66–70 %: [value/shared]. Five-sequence ledger rows: r24-t0002 on st02n5-layer-b4k-r293 and st02n5-head-b4k-r293.

Takeaway
  • Verdict: two views of one vector; 0.0322 of ratio for the join, 0.85 bit per element shared; the strongest structure found.
  • Open: keys’ heads, and whether the same linear link exists between layers.
