II.21

The forecaster’s desk

The forecaster’s desk holds four logbooks; everything else is in the archive downstairs, a slow lift away. Which logbooks should stay on the desk? This is a cache, and the simplest good answer — keep what you used most recently — is called LRU.

Seven in the morning at the harbour office, and a storm is coming. The forecaster works through the logbooks of the stations along the coast — Gull Rock (G), Seal Point (S), the Harbour buoy (H), the Lighthouse (L), the Ferry pier (F), North Cape (N), Mill Bay (M) and the Reef ®. Here is the morning’s list of asks, in order:

The desk has room for four logbooks. Every other logbook lives in the archive downstairs, and fetching one means a trip of the slow lift. Gull Rock and Seal Point come up again and again; the Reef appears once, at the very end. Which logbooks should stay on the desk?

Desk and archive

cachememory, caches & hardware
  • What it is: a small, fast store that keeps copies of some items from a big, slow one, so that most asks can be served quickly.
  • Example: the desk (four logbooks, at arm’s length) in front of the archive (every logbook, a lift ride away).
  • Easy to confuse with: the archive itself. A cache holds copies; the full collection lives in the slow store.
hit / missmemory, caches & hardware
  • A hit is an ask served from the cache: the logbook is already on the desk.
  • A miss must fetch the item from the slow store: the lift goes down and comes back.
  • The miss rate is the fraction of asks that miss. It is the number that decides how much the lift works.

Some misses cannot be avoided by anyone. The first ask for each logbook must miss, because it has never been fetched before. The storm morning asks for 8 different logbooks, so every desk, however large and however clever, makes at least 8 misses out of 24.

Everything else depends on one decision.

Takeaway
  • A cache keeps copies of a few items close at hand; a hit is served there, a miss is fetched from the slow store.
  • First-time asks always miss: 8 of the morning’s 24 asks, whatever the desk.

Who goes back down?

When the desk is full and a new logbook arrives, one logbook must go back to the archive.

evictionmemory, caches & hardware
  • What it is: removing an item from a full cache to make room for a new one.
  • The eviction policy is the rule that decides which item goes. Everything about a cache’s performance, for a given size, is in that rule.

The obvious rule bets that the recent past predicts the near future.

LRUmemory, caches & hardware
  • What it is: least recently used — evict the item whose last use lies furthest in the past.
  • Why it works: if a logbook was needed a moment ago, it will probably be needed again soon. Gull Rock is used every other ask, so it is never the least recently used.
  • Easy to confuse with: FIFO (“first in, first out”, evict whatever arrived earliest, however often it is used since) and LFU (evict the item used least often overall).

Step through the morning yourself.

☞ Things to try
  1. LRU with 4 slots: step through. Gull Rock and Seal Point never leave the desk. The morning ends with 10 misses: the 8 unavoidable first visits plus 2 (the Harbour buoy and the Lighthouse come back after being sent down).
  2. Switch to FIFO. When the Ferry pier arrives (ask 10), Gull Rock goes down just because it arrived first — and is asked for at the very next ask. Then Seal Point, then the Harbour buoy: a chain of four misses in a row. 14 misses.
  3. With 3 slots: LRU 11 misses, FIFO 16. With 6 slots both policies get close to the unavoidable 8.
Two eviction policies
LRU

Evicts the logbook unused for longest. Uses the recency of each logbook — exactly the information that predicts the next ask when work comes in bursts. Storm morning, 4 slots: 10 misses.

FIFO

Evicts the logbook that arrived earliest. Ignores how often a logbook has been used since. Storm morning, 4 slots: 14 misses.

Running themeWhich picks, versus what is inside them

LRU never opens a logbook. It knows only which logbooks were asked for and when, nothing about what is written in them. A cache can work brilliantly on items that have nothing in common with each other; the asks, not the contents, make it work. Part III meets the same distinction in an LLM (III.9 The Lucky Louis property).

ConnectionWhere the desk reappears

In Part III, a GPU keeps a small desk like this one for the memory of an LLM, and uses LRU to manage it (III.7 HiSparse: a library with a reading desk).

Takeaway
  • Eviction decides which item leaves a full cache. LRU evicts the least recently used.
  • On the storm morning with 4 slots: LRU 10 misses, FIFO 14, of which 8 are unavoidable.
  • LRU works because of the ask pattern, not because of what the items contain.
