Furrow statistics: the Anchor

The lightest dictionary there is: for each furrow of a punnet, one typical value and one spread. It charges no tax on any cherry, weighs about two cherries, and goes after exactly what the Hermit cannot see, values that drift from punnet to punnet.

Here are the first four cherries of two punnets of the same sequence, two furrows each, as 8-bit codes on an INT8 grid (illustrative numbers; the first punnet is the toy of V.1 The shape of every compressor, and its bill).

punnet of stretch 0, tokens 0 to 3 punnet of stretch 1, tokens 4096 to 4099
furrow [:, 0] 97 · 97 · 96 · 98 88 · 89 · 87 · 88
furrow [:, 1] 12 · 15 · 10 · 13 −20 · −17 · −22 · −19
furrow [:, 2] −31 · 22 · 5 · −14 18 · −40 · 3 · 27

Inside one punnet, two of the three furrows hardly move. Furrow 0 sits near 97 in the first punnet and near 88 in the second; furrow 1 near 12, then near −20. Only furrow 2 jumps around everywhere. The Hermit of V.2 Solo: the Hermit codes each furrow with one global table and must be ready for every value it ever takes. If the punnet told it “this time, expect about 97”, the leftover would be tiny.

Drop anchor on every furrow

furrow statistics“the Anchor”this project's own words
  • What it is. A dictionary holding, for each furrow of a punnet, a location (a typical value, the median) and a scale (a typical spread, which sets the width or the table of the residual code). Every element is predicted by its furrow’s location; the exact difference is coded.
  • Dictionary. Two numbers per furrow, about two cherries’ worth: w≈2. The harness also charges the compressor’s own settings, 3 bytes per furrow, which makes it w=4 in the plan’s accounting.
  • Per-cherry tax. None. Every cherry of the punnet uses the same prediction.
  • Easy to confuse with the Hermit’s tables: those are global and fixed; the Anchor’s statistics belong to one punnet (or one sequence) and follow its drift.

The stage, as analysis/plan_harness.py writes it (the Loc class, on INT8 codes):

The int16 is not pedantry. Codes [120, −120] minus locations [−100, 100] computed in int8 give [−36, 36]; the true residuals are [220, −220], and the round trip is the control that catches it (V.14 A synthetic dress rehearsal). On the toy punnet the locations are [97, 12, 5, 41] and the residuals of cherries 0 to 2 are [0, 0, −36, −1], [0, 3, 17, 3], [−1, −2, 0, −3]: two furrows collapse to a few small integers, furrow 2 stays wide. An anchor cannot predict what does not stay put.

Takeaway
  • Furrow statistics: a location and a scale per furrow per punnet; w≈2, no tax.
  • Each element is predicted by its furrow’s location; the residual is the exact difference, computed in a wider integer type.

What the Anchor buys, and when nothing

The residual’s spread is the spread within a punnet, σwithin​; the Hermit faces the spread over all punnets, σtotal​. For bell-shaped values the exchange rate (V.12 The exchange rate, applied) gives, per element,

gainloc​=21​log2​σwithin2​σtotal2​​=21​log2​1−explained by punnet locationsR2​​1​(V.3·1)
SymbolsWhat’s in this equation (3)
gain
gain of a dictionary [bits per element] — Bits per element that a dictionary’s prediction saves on the residual, compared with coding the element alone (solo), before any of the dictionary’s costs are paid. V.1
σ
standard deviation [same as the value] — Typical distance from the mean; σ2 is the variance. II.11
R2
variance explained — Share of a value’s variance a predictor accounts for; the residual keeps (1−R2)σ2. II.12

The cherry’s saving is this gain averaged over its d furrows; the cost is 8×2/b per element for the dictionary and nothing per cherry.

☞ Things to try
  1. Set the drift share to 0. The bars sit on the global mean and the Anchor saves nothing (a few hundredths, from fitting noise).
  2. Set it to 75 %. The within-punnet spread halves: one bit per element.
  3. Go to 95 %. The furrow is nearly constant inside each punnet: over two bits.

Three things decide whether the Anchor is worth its two cherries.

  • A fixed global location saves nothing for an ideal entropy coder. Entropy depends on the shape of a histogram, not on where it sits. The gain comes from locations that change, from punnet to punnet or sequence to sequence. A fixed-length code is different: centring a furrow is what lets its width be narrow, so even a global anchor helps there, and the harness charges the plan’s solo rung at global scope for exactly this reason (V.11 Two rates: entropy and fixed-length).
  • The grid can turn the gain negative. On the E4M3 grid, subtracting a location in index space can cost bits unless the location explains most of the variance; V.10 Splitting the bit fields measures it and adds a one-bit “predict or not” flag per furrow, and V.8 Context modelling never loses shows the fix that never loses.
  • The dictionary is almost free, not free. 16/b bits per element: 0.06 at b=256, 0.004 at 4096, a full bit at b=16.

On a synthetic “drift” punnet where punnet locations explain 60 % of the variance (formula gain 0.66), the measured saving was +0.64 bit per element after paying for the dictionary, the best of the four classic dictionary types there (V.14 A synthetic dress rehearsal).

Takeaway
  • The Anchor saves 21​log2​(σtotal2​/σwithin2​) per furrow: 75 % of the variance in punnet locations buys one bit.
  • It wins on drift; it gains nothing on furrows that do not drift, and can lose on a logarithmic grid without a flag.

What the campaigns found

On the Bench the Anchor turned out to be the floor rather than a step above it. The campaigns’ solo ideal, 0.7999 at layer 3 head 0, is itself computed per punnet and per furrow with the tables uncharged, so whatever drift there is between punnets already sits inside that number. Compressor 09, the best real solo compressor of V.2 Solo: the Hermit, is furrow statistics fit per punnet and charged: a centre and a width per furrow, one rANS stream per cherry, 0.8039 at stretch 4096. The 0.004 between the two is the price of shipping the statistics, and the scorecard’s verdict reads “loc alone: nothing beyond solo” (VIII.12 The scorecard). The statistics did not disappear, though: every later compressor keeps a mean and a scale per furrow underneath its reference cherries and its linear prediction (compressor 129 begins exactly so, V.7 Linear prediction across elements). One layer showed the Anchor’s natural prey: the values of the LLM’s layer 23, where about a tenth of the furrows are one-signed offsets, coded to 0.572 at 4096 tokens while every other layer sat between 0.67 and 0.74 (VIII.4 Night one: the twin ladder). And punnet size ran the wrong way for a drift story: bigger punnets always won, ever more slowly, with no drift ceiling up to 32 768 tokens (VIII.9 Reading one cherry).

SourceReceipts

Coder 09: r1-t0005 (0.80385 at 4096, 0.8045 at 512); solo ideal landmark env-t0001 (0.79989). Layer 23 values 0.572 and the one-signed furrows: r3-t0036/r3-t0040 (C1 notes/04 §3). Block-size series: r1-t0045, r1-t0042, r4-t0008. Scorecard row “loc alone”: PART-XI-SPEC §1.12.

Takeaway
  • On the Bench, charged per-furrow statistics are the solo baseline (0.804); they added no drift gain of their own.
  • They survive under every stronger stage, and they alone catch a layer whose furrows are one-signed offsets.
