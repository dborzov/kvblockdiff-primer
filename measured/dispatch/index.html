VIII.1

A dispatch from the harness

Parts I–VII describe a problem, a toolbox and a set of bets. Then a harness was built, handed to teams of AI researcher agents, and run on real Qwen3.5-2B orchards for one night and one day. This Part reports what came back: 8 raw bits became about 5, and the bytes turned out to be made of things the bets had not named.

The scenario of Part IV ends with a referee (IV.13 The harness: the referee) and the toolbox of Part V ends with a dress rehearsal on synthetic punnets (V.14 A synthetic dress rehearsal). Between them sat a plan: one evening of GPU time to dump a few long documents’ orchards, then a weekend at a desk with numpy. What happened instead took one night and one day, nobody at a keyboard wrote a compressor, and the data answered a question the napkin had not quite asked.

This Part is the report from that night and day, written for a reader who has never heard of any of it. Stage first, then picture.

What happened

The harness was built roughly as sketched: one contract every compressor passes through, one accounting rule, a round trip that must be exact. Then it was turned into a loop. An AI researcher agent reads what is known so far, writes a compressor and submits it; the harness scores it and records the trial; the agent reads the score and tries the next idea. A supervisor agent runs the environment and writes the briefs. The human steps in only to clarify the goal.

campaignthis project's own words
  • What it is. One run of that loop: several researcher agents, one harness, one set of rules, every trial recorded in a ledger nobody edits by hand. The harness, not an agent, keeps the score.
  • Example. C1, the night of 29–30 October 2026: four agents, one head of one KV layer, no leash yet. C2, 30 October: about twenty-one agents, all six KV layers, the leash in force.
  • Easy to confuse with a trial (one recorded test of one compressor on one scenario) and with the harness itself (the referee the campaign runs through).

The data was real: the value half of Qwen3.5-2B’s orchard over thirty long coding-agent transcripts, 20.9 GB of bytes (VIII.2 The data on the table). The rules were real too, and the human clarified them twice during the first night, the second time to close a loophole the search had found (VIII.3 The rules of the game, as scenarios).

Takeaway
  • The plan was a weekend of numpy. What ran was a harness driving two campaigns of AI researcher agents: 126 compressors, about 390 trials.
  • A campaign is one run of the loop: agents propose compressors, the harness scores them, the ledger remembers.
  • C1 measured one head of one KV layer through the night; C2 measured all six KV layers the next day.

The headline

A raw value is 8 bits. Coding every element alone with the best per-furrow statistics a punnet can offer, the ideal is 0.778 of that, about 6.22 bits (solo coding, which V.2 Solo: the Hermit guessed within 0.002). The best verified compressor with a per-punnet dictionary reached 0.636 at a stretch of 4096 tokens on the two development sequences, 0.630 on five sequences of the larger set, and 0.618 at a stretch of 16 384 tokens. In the site’s unit, bits per element: 8, then 6.2, then 5.1, then 4.9.

SourceReceipts

Throughout this Part, “notes” and “spec” name the verification notes and the pinned-number sheet kept with the site’s source under experiment_repos/kv-compress-roofline/; trial ids such as env-t0096 are rows of the campaigns’ ledgers. Solo ideal 0.77811 is the landmark of the layer screen at 4096 tokens (notes/01 §B2). Best real solo coder 0.8039: coder 09, r1-t0005. C1’s ladder top 0.6731: coder 34, r1-t0042 (layer 3, head 0, no read budget). C2: 0.63639 and 0.62970, coder 121, env-t0096 (two sequences of target 03; five of target 02); 0.61768 at 16,384 tokens, coder 120, env-t0097 (two sequences). The block floor ≈0.60 is an exact-model extrapolation, not a coder (spec §1.10). Counts: 387 ledger trials (C1 218, C2 169), 126 coders (notes/00).

In the scenario’s words: the score is the MDL ratio, dictionary share plus delta share (IV.8 The score: the MDL ratio). The napkin of Part V expected values to end near a delta share of 0.75–0.85, with any gain coming from keys. The sum came in at 0.636 for values: 1.1 to 1.3 bits below solo. How it splits between dictionary share and delta share turned out to say where the bytes were filed rather than how much the cherries share; VIII.9 Reading one cherry explains why.

Where did the bits go? Three places, each with its own page. Repeats: in a stretch of 4096 tokens many value cherries are near-copies of an earlier cherry, usually the same token appearing again, and the best compressors store the earlier one and point at it (VIII.4 Night one: the twin ladder). Geometry: within one cherry the elements predict each other through a rule fixed by the LLM’s own weights, a rule that transfers from one text to another (VIII.5 Two kinds of structure). A remainder that no per-punnet compressor reaches, because it is the text’s long memory, what the LLM was attending to thousands of tokens back (VIII.10 Where the bits come from).

Repeats belong to the text; geometry belongs to the LLM.

Takeaway
  • Raw 8 bits → solo 6.2 → 5.1 at a stretch of 4096 tokens → 4.9 at 16 384; ratios 1 → 0.778 → 0.636 → 0.618.
  • That is 1.1–1.3 bits below solo, on values, where the napkin expected almost nothing.
  • The bits came from repeats of earlier tokens, from geometry set by the weights, and from a remainder that is the text’s memory.

CarefulWhat these numbers are, and are not
  • Values only. C2 never touched keys; C1 coded keys for one head of each KV layer and found they compress like values (VIII.4 Night one: the twin ladder).
  • One LLM, Qwen3.5-2B, and one kind of text: the author’s own coding-agent transcripts, which repeat themselves more than prose does.
  • One number format, FP8 E4M3, on the six KV layers of a hybrid LLM.
  • Mostly two sequences. Nearly every result comes from the two development sequences of a three-sequence set; the best compressors reached five sequences at the very end. C2 recorded no holdout score.
  • Three grades of number. Verified: every cherry rebuilt bit for bit by a separate decoder process from the stored bytes alone (verified). Code-length estimate: a probability table’s code length computed on the data, no compressor built (code-length estimate). Bound: a limit on a whole family. Each page says which is which, and names one-window and two-sequence numbers as such.
Takeaway
  • Three pages of stage, eight of picture, two of meaning, two in bits; the map links them in reading order.
  • Every number here is values only, one LLM, one kind of text, FP8, six KV layers, mostly two sequences.
  • Verified, code-length estimate and bound are three different kinds of number; the pages say which is which.
