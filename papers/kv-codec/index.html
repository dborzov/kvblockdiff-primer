IX.6

KVFetcher (2026)

A stored prefix’s orchard laid out as a video, so the GPU’s video chips can compress it for the trip across a network. The closest thing in print to a fallen-apple measurement, and further from one than the first draft thought.

Film a harbour with a camera on a tripod. Frame after frame, almost every pixel is what it was a moment ago. A video compressor sends the first frame, then for every later frame only how it differs from the one before: predictive coding with the previous frame as the prediction. That is why an hour of a quiet harbour fits in a small file. Replace the harbour with an orchard, and the frames with tokens. That is the whole idea of KVFetcher (the system the first draft called KV-Codec). Its appeal is obvious: if consecutive tokens’ cherries look alike, the video compressor will find it, and its ratio becomes a reading of fallen-apple similarity. This page asks how good a reading it is.

The idea, by example

KVFetcher lives on the network-fetch road of IX.1 › The data path. A long prefix, a document or a system prompt, was prefilled once and its orchard stored on another machine. A new sequence that starts with the same prefix fetches that orchard over the network instead of recomputing it. What matters is the time to the first answer token.

Take a toy orchard of three layers, 8 tokens and 8 elements per token, v.shape == (8, 3, 1, 8). The compressor, step by step:

  1. Round. Every number is rounded to a small integer. The only lossy step, and the reason “lossless accuracy” in the paper does not mean lossless on this site.
  2. Lay out. Each token’s 8 numbers become one small picture, a 4 × 2 frame; consecutive tokens become consecutive frames; the three layers are the frame’s three colour planes. Numbers of different KV heads are never mixed, and the exact picture shape is found by an offline search. (For LWM-7B a summary gives an 8 × 512 picture, exactly the 4 096 key numbers of one token at one layer; the one-token-per-frame reading is our inference.)
  3. Code. A standard H.265 video compressor runs in its lossless mode. For each small patch of pixels it predicts from the same place in the previous frame (the previous token) or from neighbouring pixels of the same frame (neighbouring elements of the same token) and stores the exact residual.
  4. Decode. The GPU’s video-decoding chip, not its compute cores, decodes runs of about 10 000 tokens while the next run is still on the network.

In our words: lossless predictive coding of rounded cherries with a borrowed predictor, “previous token, same element” plus “neighbouring element, same token”. The first is fallen-apple prediction at Δ=1. The second is structure inside one cherry, the territory of V.7 Linear prediction across elements.

Takeaway
  • Round to integers (lossy), lay each token out as a frame, let a hardware video compressor predict from the previous token and from neighbouring elements, decode on the video chip.
  • Its inter-frame prediction is fallen-apple prediction at token distance 1, at rounded precision.

What they assume

  1. Consecutive tokens are alike within a furrow. Stated as the motivation for the video analogy; never measured in bits. This is the hypothesis of VII.3 Fallen-apple similarity (token axis), assumed true.
  2. The LLM tolerates coarse rounding. Benchmark accuracy is the yardstick, as in every lossy page of this Part.
  3. The road is a network fetch, read in bulk. A run of thousands of tokens is decoded as one video; nobody asks for one token.
  4. The video chip is idle. A serving stack leaves the GPU’s media engines unused, so decoding there is free of compute-core contention.
Takeaway
  • Assumed, not measured: fallen-apple similarity at distance 1.
  • Known: a bulk road, a tolerant LLM, an idle video chip.

What they show

their measurement value where verifiable?
time to first token, against earlier methods up to 3.51× shorter, “with lossless accuracy” abstract their rig
compression ratio 11.9× “while keeping high accuracy”, rounding and coding together ablation, earlier version, via a summary not decomposable from what we could read
ratio against CacheGen 2.17× higher latest version, via a truncated summary —
swapping 50 % of the numbers across 32 heads ratio 2.4× worse layout study, via a summary a control; informative
rounding precision (bits per number) not found — —

Where the framing flatters: “lossless accuracy” is not lossless; only the rounded integers are rebuilt exactly. And the headline ratio hides its parts. If the rounding went from 16 to 8 bits, it alone gives 2×, leaving about 6× to lossless coding of 8-bit integers, far more than anything else in this Part reaches; that suggests coarser rounding, but the bit width could not be found. Without it, 11.9× says nothing about predictability.

The head-swap study is the one result we keep. It is a control of the element arrangement, and it shows that layout is half the compressor. Its twin is missing: shuffle the token order and see how much of the ratio survives. Since the swap left the token order untouched and still cost a factor 2.4, a large share of the gain may come from inside each token, not from its neighbour.

Running themeTrust, but shuffle

KVFetcher ran half of a control (scramble the elements) and not the other half (scramble the tokens). The half it ran is informative; the half it skipped is the one we needed, and the one the harness runs as its shuffle control (IV.13 The harness: the referee).

Takeaway
  • Rounded first, so lossy for us; the ratio bundles rounding, token-axis prediction and within-token prediction.
  • Keep the layout lesson; leave the ratio out of every estimate.

What it says about our scenario

Builds on.

  • Layout decides the compressor: what sits next to what is the first design choice, before any statistics (VI.5 The access pattern decides the layout). The 2.4× under a head swap is a published instance.
  • Prediction across elements inside one cherry is real: the campaigns’ linear prediction across elements, head 0 predicting head 1, saved 0.013–0.034 of ratio on top of a reference tree, and survived the shuffle control (VIII.5 Two kinds of structure, V.7 Linear prediction across elements). KVFetcher’s within-frame prediction is the lossy cousin.

Conflicts with. The token-axis bet. The campaigns measured fallen-apple similarity on the Bench’s values and found it weak except in the middle layers: the adjacent token carries 0.058 bit per element of mutual information at KV layer 0, 0.31 at layers 1 and 2; joining two adjacent tokens into one cherry made the ratio worse by 0.037; and the reference trees pointed at far repeats of the same token, not at neighbours (VII.3 Fallen-apple similarity (token axis), VII.6 Echo similarity (repeats), VIII.8 Sound, time and bits). We believe the campaigns: they count exact bits on an 8-bit grid with a shuffle control, where KVFetcher reports a bundled ratio at an unknown rounding. The two can both be right if KVFetcher’s gain comes mostly from inside the frame and from rounding, which its own head-swap control hints at.

Suggests.

  • The missing control, run on their data: the same pipeline with the token order shuffled across texts. If the 11.9× survives, the token axis was never the source.
  • A video-chip decoder is a free decoder for bulk roads only; on the miss path one cherry decoded cold cannot wait for a run of 10 000 frames (IV.9 The leash). Nothing to borrow there.
  • A curve of bits saved over Δ per layer, which the campaigns measured only at a few distances, would say whether KVFetcher’s frames would do better in the middle layers (X.4 Next in the harness).
Running themeBits are the unit

A ratio like 11.9× cannot be converted into “bits saved per element by the previous token”. Until a paper reports bits per element for each predictor separately, it can only hint at fallen-apple similarity, not measure it. The campaigns’ 0.058 and 0.31 bit per element are the measurement.

Also on II.1 · II.2 · II.4 · III.12 · IV.5 · IV.11 · VI.11 · VII.1 · VII.5 · VIII.6 · VIII.14 · XII.9
Takeaway
  • Builds on: layout first; within-cherry prediction is real.
  • Conflicts with: the token-axis bet, which the Bench measured as weak; we believe the exact bits.
  • Suggests: the shuffle control they skipped.

Their words, ours

their word ours where the meanings differ
KV cache, stored prefix the orchard of a prefix one sequence’s, stored and fetched whole

chunk a run of about 10 000 tokens, decoded as one video far longer than any stretch, and read front to back
channel element one position inside a head vector
lossless accuracy lossy, with benchmark scores unchanged the rounded integers come back exactly; the BF16 numbers do not
codec, video codec the video compressor (H.265 in lossless mode) —
inter-frame prediction fallen-apple similarity prediction at Δ=1 at rounded precision
intra-frame prediction prediction across elements inside one cherry the lossy cousin of linear prediction
