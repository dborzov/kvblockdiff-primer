IX.9

KVTC (2026)

The JPEG recipe applied to the orchard. Rotate to the data’s own axes, give the big axes bits and the small ones none, then pack. Up to 20× smaller, lossy. Its lossless shadow is the KLT bound, and the two numbers grow at very different speeds.

A JPEG photo is not stored pixel by pixel. Each 8 × 8 patch is turned into a mix of patterns, from smooth to busy. The smooth patterns carry most of the picture and get many bits; the busy ones get few or none, because the eye does not miss them. Then a general-purpose compressor packs what is left. KVTC does the same to an orchard: turn each vector onto the axes the data prefers, spend bits where the spread is, drop the rest, pack. It reports up to 20× smaller orchards with accuracy kept, and 40× or more in some cases. It is lossy. The question for us is what the same recipe is worth when nothing may be dropped.

The idea, by example

KVTC lives on the bulk road of IX.1 › The data path: whole orchards compressed for storage and offload, read back together. On a toy vector of 64 elements, v[t, l, h, :] with head_dim = 64:

  1. Fit the axes once. On a short calibration run, collect key and value vectors, stack them, X.shape == (n, 64), and compute the axes of greatest spread, np.linalg.eigh(np.cov(X.T)): a KLT (the paper says PCA; same thing). Computed once per LLM and reused for every orchard: a dictionary of global scope (IV.10 Who pays for the dictionary: scope).
  2. Rotate. Each vector is described by its 64 coordinates along those axes.
  3. Spend bits where the spread is. A dynamic program decides how many bits each axis gets for a given budget. Axes with little spread get zero: they are dropped.
  4. Round and pack. The coordinates are rounded and the bytes packed with DEFLATE (the zip format), run on the GPU through NVIDIA’s nvCOMP library.

Takeaway
  • KVTC = a KLT fitted once + bit allocation that drops small axes + rounding + DEFLATE.
  • The axes are a global dictionary, free at serving time; the packer is a stream.

What they assume

  1. The orchard’s spectrum is steep. Transform coding pays only when a few axes hold most of the variance; the paper assumes it and the 20× confirms it for a lossy purpose.
  2. A global basis suffices. Axes fit once per LLM on a calibration run serve every orchard. The same bet as Lexico’s one dictionary per LLM (IX.10 Lexico (2024)).
  3. Accuracy is the yardstick. GSM8K, MMLU, LongBench, RULER: what the LLM can do without.
  4. The road is bulk. Whole orchards for storage and offload, where a stream packer is natural.

Two details we could read only in secondary summaries: that RoPE is undone before the PCA (the KVQuant lesson, IX.8 KVQuant (2024)), and that the first few and most recent tokens are handled separately.

Takeaway
  • Known: steep spectra, a global basis, accuracy as the measure, bulk reads.

Both the lossy compressor and its lossless shadow start from the same list: the variances λ along the KLT axes, largest first. They do different things with it. A lossy compressor pours a “water level” over the list. Each axis above the water gets bits in proportion to the log of how far it sticks out; each axis below gets none:

A lossless compressor may not drop anything. Every axis still costs bits down to the stored grid, including the grid’s own rounding noise ν, which sits on every axis. The rotation saves only the unevenness of the spread:

ReminderThe transform coding gain (II.16)

Rotating to the KLT axes and coding each axis on its own saves 21​log2​(AM/GM) bits per number, where AM and GM are the ordinary and geometric averages of the variances, measured on data where every furrow has first been scaled to the same spread. Equal variances give 0. See II.16 What the rotation buys.

☞ Things to try
  1. Steepness 2.0, error 5 %: the lossy compressor keeps 20 of the 64 axes at 0.40 bits per number on average, 40×. The same rotation, losslessly, saves 1.98 bits per number.
  2. Steepness 1.5: lossy 15×, lossless 1.09 bits. Steepness 2.5: lossy 91×, lossless 2.99 bits. The lossy number grows like a ratio, the lossless one like a logarithm.
  3. At steepness 2.5, switch the grid noise to 1 %: the lossless gain falls to 2.37 bits. The lossy compressor does not care; it dropped those axes anyway.

Where the framing flatters: a raw spectrum is steep partly because furrows have different sizes (KIVI’s big key furrows). Per-furrow statistics price that in without rotating; the rotation’s own gain is measured on standardized data, and nobody has published that spectrum for 8-bit orchards. We could not check what the 20× counts, whether tokens kept at full precision, the stored axes and the DEFLATE framing are all inside it.

Takeaway
  • Lossy: drop the axes below the water level; ratios grow fast with a steep spectrum.
  • Lossless: keep every axis; save only 21​log2​(AM/GM) on standardized data, with the grid’s noise on every axis.

What it says about our scenario

Builds on.

  • The KLT as a dictionary, with its need for a spiky spectrum (V.4 KLT: the Compass, at work, II.16 What the rotation buys), and the bound-before-building rule of V.13 Bounds before building: compute the AM/GM on standardized data first.
  • The grid noise ν on every axis (V.10 Splitting the bit fields): a lossless compressor must reproduce it.

Conflicts with. The campaigns tried rotations on the Bench’s values and they lost. Fixed rotations (DCT, Hadamard, random) cost +0.28 to +0.32 bit per element against no rotation (code-length estimate, two heads); rotating and then rounding costs about 0.25 bit per element, close to the lattice constant 0.2546; and the same-index correlation across elements (0.07 at one middle layer) gave a DCT nothing to work with (VIII.7 Codebooks and rotations). What won was linear prediction across elements, which subtracts a guess and never rounds the vector. We believe the campaigns for lossless coding on an 8-bit grid: KVTC’s recipe pays a rounding tax after the rotation that the lossy version never has to count. KVTC’s steep spectrum can still be real; it is spent on dropping axes, which we may not do.

Suggests.

  • The standardized spectrum of the Bench’s values, per layer, as a desk bound: 21​log2​(AM/GM) with ν added, against the 0.25-bit rounding tax. If the bound is under the tax, no lossless rotation can pay (V.13 Bounds before building).
  • If it is over: lifting turns the rotation into an integer-reversible one with no rounding tax (V.9 Lifting: how a rotation becomes lossless, IX.13 › Rotations on integers: lifting and PLUS); the campaigns did not try lifted rotations.
Running themeThe access pattern decides the form

KVTC compresses whole orchards for storage and offload, where everything is read back together, and a stream packer like DEFLATE is natural. On the miss path, one cherry of 512 bytes decoded cold under the leash, the same packer would force decoding from the start of a stream. The road decides the packer before the statistics do.

Takeaway
  • Builds on: the KLT bound, the grid noise.
  • Conflicts with: rotations lost on the Bench by 0.25–0.32 bit; prediction without rounding won. We believe the exact bits.
  • Suggests: the standardized spectrum as a bound, and lifting if it clears the tax.
